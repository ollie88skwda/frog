The Gang · Council Verdict

Should we build SBL's v1 as specced?

Question: Is the v1 MVP scoped/sequenced correctly for a solo dev, what should be built/validated first, what are the biggest risks, and is deferring the "Findings" correlation engine to v1.x a mistake?

Verdict

Where the gang agrees (high confidence)

  • The Findings engine is the actual product; conditions logging is the behavior that feeds it. Everything else (logging, RIR, ghost prefill, program builder) is table stakes the incumbents already do well. (Mean Mark, Simple Simon, What-If Willy, Clueless Chris, Do-It Dora — all five.)
  • Deferring Findings to v1.x is a mistake. At launch you'd be a generic tracker with extra logging friction — the data-entry tax of the moat with none of the payoff. Users log on faith for weeks, see nothing, churn. Ship at least a primitive/teaser Findings from day one.
  • v1 is too big and mis-prioritized for a solo dev. Strong consensus to cut from v1: program builder, joint-action controlled vocabulary, progression engine, seat/setup recall (Simple Simon also cuts cloud sync).
  • Validate the correlation engine before building the app — on a spreadsheet with mocked + your own real training data. "Build the magic trick before the stage."
  • Conditions logging must be dead-simple (a few taps) or nobody sustains it — and then there's no data.

Where the gang clashes

  • Habit-first vs. proof-first. What-If Willy says build the conditions-logging habit first (assumes the engine will work). Mean Mark / Do-It Dora / Simple Simon say prove the math works first — it may produce only noise on tiny, noisy data. Same coin, opposite starting move.
  • Ambition. What-If Willy alone wants to frame it as a platform (n-of-1 experimentation beyond the gym — running, cuts, sleep, biohacking). Everyone else says narrow and validate before dreaming.
  • Cloud sync in v1? Simple Simon cuts it; the others leave it.

Blind spots the peer review caught

  • Statistical validity. No advisor seriously stress-tested the science: n-of-1 over a few dozen noisy sessions invites confounding, multiple-comparisons false positives, and reverse causality. Spurious "findings" shown to a rigor-obsessed audience is a credibility risk. Needs guardrails: minimum n, confidence display, honest "correlation ≠ causation."
  • Garbage-in. Self-reported sleep/stress/carbs are noisy and biased — bad inputs doom Findings even if the math is perfect. Input accuracy is a data-validity threat, not just friction.
  • Retention, distribution, business model. Unexamined: who the user is and how they're acquired, App-Store economics for a niche audience, switching cost / history import from Hevy, and the obvious competitive response — Hevy just adds a "conditions" field.

The recommendation

Don't build v1 as specced. Reorder around the thesis.

  1. Validate the engine first (days, ~zero cost): mock ~8 weeks of sessions + use your own real log in a spreadsheet, write the correlation logic, and judge whether it yields a genuine "huh, interesting" insight that survives basic statistical guardrails. This could save months.
  2. If it holds, build a deliberately narrow v1: fast logging (ghost prefill, RIR) + dead-simple conditions capture + a visible, honest Findings surface from day one (heuristic/teaser is fine: "3 sessions until your first finding," with confidence + causation caveats).
  3. Defer the tracker table-stakes (program builder, joint-action vocab, progression engine, seat recall); reconsider whether sync is even v1.

Keep the lab-notebook design identity (it's good and cheap) — but flip the feature priority so the differentiator is present at launch.

The one thing to do first

Before any app code: open a spreadsheet, enter a mix of mocked 8-week data and your own real training sessions with conditions, write the correlation query, and decide honestly — is the output a revelation or noise dressed as science? If revelation → build the narrow v1 around it. If noise → the premise needs rework, and you just saved yourself months.

Mean Mark · The Contrarian

The fatal flaw: you're building a tracker for people who already have one (Hevy/Strong = muscle memory). High switching cost for a promissory note. Deferring Findings guts it — the differentiator IS the engine; everything else is table stakes a solo dev won't beat in v1. Conditions logging is pure tax until Findings ships; users won't log on faith, then Findings launches on low-n data and surfaces spurious correlations — a liability that looks unscientific to a rigor audience. Scope too big AND mis-prioritized. Build first: a dead-simple conditions+Findings prototype on your OWN training data. If correlations are garbage (biology is noisy, n is tiny), the premise is dead. Validate the thesis, not the app.

Simple Simon · First Principles

Lifters don't think "I want to log conditions" — they think "why did today suck when last week felt easy?" The product is the answer to that; everything else is plumbing. The differentiator is the Findings engine, and you've deferred the one thing that IS the product. At launch: all the data-entry tax, none of the payoff — log for 4 days, no insight, quit. Cut from v1: program builder, joint-action vocab, progression engine, seat recall, cloud sync. Keep: fast logging, conditions capture, a primitive Findings view (even hardcoded heuristics) — insight on day 10 not week 12. Validate the math in a spreadsheet on your own log first; if it doesn't yield "huh, interesting," the premise is dead.

What-If Willy · The Expansionist

Everyone will tell you to cut scope; I'll tell you the opposite about the one thing that matters. Conditions logging IS the product — build it first, not buried at step 5. The differentiator is the habit of logging conditions; Findings monetizes a behavior you bootstrap from day one. Upside being undervalued: this isn't a lifting app, it's a personal n-of-1 experimentation engine — extends to running, cuts, sleep, biohacking. "Lab notebook for your body" is a platform; gym is the wedge. Don't defer Findings entirely — ship a teaser (one hardcoded insight at week 3 = addiction loop + screenshot-able viral moment = free growth). Cut program builder and joint-action tags from v1.

Clueless Chris · The Outsider

The "lab notebook" idea is the only interesting part — but v1 is mostly a normal tracker, so the idea is buried; a normal person sees a tracker that also nags about sleep. Build the one thing nobody else does: conditions logging, dead simple (more than a few taps = people skip it = no data). Confusing assumption: serious people logging six extra things every session forever before any payoff — that's a lot of homework. And how do I score "stress 1-10"? Says who? Missing: a reason to come back before Findings exists. Delaying Findings is a real mistake — even a dumb version ("your last 5 workouts next to how you slept") lets people SEE the point.

Do-It Dora · The Executor

You don't validate a correlation engine by building a tracker and waiting six weeks — validate it now in a spreadsheet before writing code. Mock 8 weeks (sleep, carbs, caffeine, RIR, e1RM), write the correlation logic, look at the output: revelation or noise dressed as science? If a solo dev can't make it land on hand-picked data, it never will on messy low-n data. v1 too big, differentiator buried. Cut: program builder, progression engine, joint-action tags, seat/setup recall. Ship: fast logging + conditions + ghost prefill + a visible running Findings teaser. Deferring Findings guts you. Monday: open Sheets, fake data, write the query. If it lands, build around it; if not, you saved four months.
